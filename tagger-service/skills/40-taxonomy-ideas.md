# Taxonomy ideas for the document

Besides filling the fields, suggest how the document could be found and filed. These are
ideas for the historian; nothing is saved from them by itself.

- subject_tags: 3 to 8 subject headings a researcher would search for. Reuse a heading the
  site already uses (listed under "Subject headings already used on the site"), in its exact
  spelling, whenever one fits; add a new heading only when none does, in the same style (like
  Library of Congress subject headings: "Camping", "Youth organizations", "Uniforms",
  "Order of the Arrow"), without dates or place names, which have their own fields.
- people: people the document names, with their role in brackets when it says so:
  "Chuck Champe (camp director)". At most 10. Never guess a name.
- events: events the document names or describes: "1952 summer camp season",
  "Council merger". At most 6.
- not_in_index: councils, camps and lodges the text names that are NOT among the candidates
  you were given: {"type": "camp", "name": "Camp Woodland", "evidence": "a short quote"}. These
  tell the historians what the index is missing. Leave out anything you matched to a candidate.
- extra_tags: other details worth recording that the site has no field for yet, named the
  Dublin Core way where one fits (Language, Format, Rights, Coverage, Relation) or plainly
  (Volume and issue, Edition, Number of pages):
  {"name": "Volume and issue", "value": "Vol. 12, No. 3", "why": "printed on the masthead"}.
  At most 6, and only values the document actually shows.
- filing_notes: one to three sentences on how to file it: which category and why, which fields
  matter most for this kind of document, and anything a reviewer should decide.

# Taxonomy ideas for the whole archive

When asked about the whole archive, you get numbers about how its documents are tagged today.
Base every idea on those numbers and say which numbers led to it. The Subject field holds
several headings joined with "; ". Prefer a small, controlled list of headings over many
near-duplicate spellings, and say which existing spellings each heading would replace. Keep what works: the State, Council, Camp, Lodge, Start Date and End
Date taxonomies and the archive's META fields stay unless the numbers show a field is unused or
confusing. Mark anything that needs the historians' decision as such, and never propose
deleting documents or tags; propose merging or renaming instead.
